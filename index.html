Celestial Lattice Scatterer

A fixed cubic or planar lattice of spherical scatterers. Choose whether the moving particle is an injected projectile or one of the lattice balls itself.

Visualization mode 3D renders the full cubic lattice and spatial trajectory. 2D uses a planar square lattice and a true two-dimensional collision calculation. 3D spatial lattice
Moving entity Injected projectile keeps the original model: a new white ball starts at the origin. Lattice ball makes one lattice sphere itself the moving particle, removes it from the static lattice, and gives it the same radius as every other sphere. Injected projectile
Center-to-center distance between neighboring lattice spheres. It controls the lattice scale and collision geometry.
Radius of every fixed lattice sphere. In Lattice ball mode, the moving sphere uses this exact same radius.
Radius of the injected moving ball. Collision occurs when center-to-center distance equals obstacle radius plus projectile radius. Locked to obstacle radius in Lattice ball mode.
Initial speed of the moving particle. Perfectly elastic fixed-scatterer collisions conserve its speed, so v changes time-of-flight but not the geometric path.
Initial direction angle in the x-y plane, measured counterclockwise from +x. In 3D this version still starts with zero z-component.
Target number of collision events to compute and animate. The table records every collision reached.
Number of lattice indices retained on each side of the origin. A finite rendering/search domain replaces the mathematically infinite lattice.
Delay used to reveal one collision segment at a time during the cinematic run. Smaller values make the animation faster.
Keyboard navigation Use W or ↑ to move the view +Y, S or ↓ to move −Y, A or ← to move −X, and D or → to move +X. In 3D, Q moves +Z and E moves −Z. Hold Shift for faster movement. This moves the camera view, not the simulated particle.
