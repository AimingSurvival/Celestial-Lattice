Celestial Lattice Scatterer

What the controls mean
Spacing a: distance between neighboring lattice-sphere centers.
Obstacle radius: radius of each fixed spherical scatterer.
Projectile radius: radius of the moving ball. Collision occurs when center-to-center distance reaches the sum of the two radii.
Speed v: initial speed of the projectile. In this fixed-scatterer model it stays constant after elastic reflections, so changing it changes time-of-flight, not the geometric path.
Angle θ: initial direction angle in the x-y plane, measured from the +x axis.
Collisions: number of collision events to calculate, unless the projectile exits the finite lattice first.
Lattice half-extent: number of lattice cells from the origin in each ±x, ±y and ±z direction. The rendered lattice therefore contains (2H+1)³ spheres.
Animation: delay between displayed collision segments in milliseconds. It changes visualization speed only.
Run simulation: recomputes the trajectory from the current parameters.
Step: advances the projectile by one collision at a time.
Reset: returns the displayed projectile to the origin without deleting the computed result.
Visualization: blue spheres are the lattice, the pink line is the trajectory, yellow points mark collision locations, and the white sphere is the projectile.
Physics: collisions are perfectly elastic and specular against fixed scatterers, so speed is conserved while direction changes.

A fixed cubic lattice of spherical scatterers. The projectile undergoes perfectly elastic, specular reflections and we record its location after each collision.
